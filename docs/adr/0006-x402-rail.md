# x402 as the second rail: pay-per-call listings, paid directly to the seller, verified receipts

[ADR-0002](0002-usdc-chains-and-escrow.md) decision 8 names three rails: escrow for agreed deliverables, x402 for fixed-price and metered calls, a direct transfer for trusted work; "a paid call never funds a job". [ADR-0001](0001-v2-scope.md) decision 7 had Sidequest host the x402 seller route. [ADR-0003](0003-nostr-native.md) since made Sidequest an index of signed events with no accounts, which that hosted route does not fit. This record decides what the x402 rail is in the Nostr-native product.

## What x402 is today (checked 13 November 2026)

- **The specification** lives at the x402 Foundation, [`github.com/x402-foundation/x402`](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402) (`coinbase/x402` is the old home), read at commit `1a08434` (13 November 2026). Protocol version 2 (`specs/x402-specification-v2.md`, v2.0 of 9 December 2025) is current: CAIP-2 networks, `PaymentRequired` with `resource` and `accepts`, `PaymentPayload` with `accepted` and a scheme payload, `SettlementResponse`, and a facilitator's `POST /verify`, `POST /settle`, `GET /supported`. Over HTTP (`specs/transports-v2/http.md`) the three are base64 JSON headers: `PAYMENT-REQUIRED` on the 402, `PAYMENT-SIGNATURE` on the paid retry, `PAYMENT-RESPONSE` on the answer. Version 1 used `X-PAYMENT` and `X-PAYMENT-RESPONSE` and network names such as `base-sepolia`; facilitators still accept it, Sidequest speaks only v2.
- **Schemes**: `exact` (a fixed amount; on EVM by ERC-3009 `transferWithAuthorization` by default, Permit2 or ERC-7710 otherwise, `specs/schemes/exact/scheme_exact_evm.md`), `upto` (a metered amount up to a maximum, Permit2), `batch-settlement`, and `auth-capture` (v1.1 of 18 August 2026: a hold, capture, void, refund and reclaim lifecycle on commerce-payments' `AuthCaptureEscrow`, with client-bounded fees). `exact` has no refund and no fee: "the Facilitator cannot modify the amount or destination".
- **MCP**: `specs/transports-v2/mcp.md` carries the challenge as a tool error's `structuredContent` and the payment in `_meta["x402/payment"]`. **Discovery**: facilitators may run a "Bazaar" (`GET /discovery/resources`, `specs/extensions/bazaar.md`). **Receipts**: an optional extension (`extension-offer-and-receipt.md`) lets the seller sign offers and receipts; its wire shape is marked unstable.
- **Hosted facilitators**, by what each states:
  - x402.org's (`https://x402.org/facilitator/supported`, queried 13 November 2026): `exact`, `upto` and `batch-settlement` on Base Sepolia (`eip155:84532`), and testnets of Solana, Algorand, Aptos, Stellar, Hedera and XRPL; **no Arc**, no mainnet.
  - Circle's Facilitator Service ([developers.circle.com/facilitator-service](https://developers.circle.com/facilitator-service), "Supported networks" page, read 13 November 2026): `exact` (ERC-3009) and `upto` on **Arc** (`eip155:5042`, testnet `eip155:5042002`, USDC `0x3600…0000`), **Base** (`eip155:8453`; Base Sepolia `exact` only) and Polygon PoS; x402 v2's `/verify`, `/settle` and `/supported` under `https://api.circle.com/v1/facilitator/x402/`; a Circle API key to settle, or a keyless trial on a per-`payTo` allowance authenticated by an EIP-712 seller proof; it screens both parties and pays gas. Circle's Gateway Nanopayments is a second facilitator that batches settlement, so a payment has no transaction of its own.
  - Coinbase's CDP facilitator ([docs.cdp.coinbase.com/x402](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/x402/seller/facilitator), read through search on 13 November 2026, not queried): Base and Base Sepolia with `exact`, `upto` and `batch-settlement`, and other mainnets; **no Arc**; API keys required.
  - Independent ones claim Arc (Arcus, TRDEFI's Arc facilitator, a self-hostable one); none was checked.

## Decisions

1. **A pay-per-call listing is a NIP-99 listing** (kind 30402) that names an x402 resource the seller hosts and the wallet it pays: `["sq-x402", <https URL>]` and `["sq-pay-to", <lowercase CAIP-10 account>]` beside its exact price (`sq-price`, required on this rail, in a registered asset on the `payTo`'s network). The reader is strict: an escrow listing with either tag, or a pay-per-call listing without a price, is refused. Its rail is the listing's (`IndexListing.rail`), never a job's: `Terms.rail` stays `escrow`, and nothing on this rail makes or funds a job.
2. **The seller's own server is paid, through a facilitator it chose.** The resource answers 402 with an `exact` offer, verifies and settles the buyer's ERC-3009 authorization through its facilitator, does the work and answers. **Sidequest is not in the payment path and holds nothing**: no Sidequest server signs, relays, verifies or settles an x402 payment, and Sidequest's relayer, which pays gas for escrow steps, does nothing on this rail.
3. **What Sidequest adds**: discovery (pay-per-call listings in the index, searchable by rail); the wallet link check (the index labels a listing's `payTo` linked only while it is the seller's winning verified wallet link, and the agent gateway refuses to quote one that is not); and receipts as reputation input (decision 4). The gateway's `x402_quote` checks that the seller's live challenge asks exactly what the listing says (the `payTo`, the amount, the asset and network, ERC-3009, a timeout of at most a day, the resource's origin) and answers the typed data the operator's wallet signs; `x402_receipt` publishes the receipt. An agent cannot pay: its key holds no money and there is no `pay` scope until Nod's spending delegations.
4. **A payment receipt is a Sidequest event** (kind 8420, free in Sidequest's block): the listing's address and the version paid, the resource, the paying wallet, the amount, the asset and the settlement transaction, signed by the buyer or by their agent under a grant with the `accept` scope. The index labels it **verified** only when the confirmed transaction holds an ERC-20 transfer of exactly its amount of its asset from its payer to that version's `payTo`, the amount is at least the version's price, and the payer's wallet is currently linked to the receipt's author (or to the operator of the unrevoked grant the author signed under). Verification is a **fact table filled by a poller**: the chain log poller asks the chain, once a minute, about each transaction a receipt names (`x402_tx_checks`, `x402_transfers`, keyed by network and transaction hash), and stores what the chain says, never a verdict; whether a receipt is verified is decided by each read from those rows and the events. So the labels are the same in any arrival order and after a rebuild from events and the chain. A listing shows how many distinct transactions its verified receipts name.
5. **No refunds and no disputes on this rail.** `exact` moves the money once; the seller can take it whether or not the call worked. The website and the gateway say so in those words. Work that needs a deadline and a review belongs on the escrow rail.
6. **The fee: none on this rail (recommended; Kris's decision).** Escrow takes 5% on chain because the operator contract sits between the money and the seller. An `exact` payment goes straight to `payTo`, so a fee needs Sidequest in the path. The options:
   - **No fee** (recommended): the rail is discovery and reputation that bring buyers to escrowed work, where the fee is; it matches ADR-0002 decision 6 ("paid calls … carry no fee").
   - **A facilitator-level split**: `payTo` a splitter contract, or the `auth-capture` scheme's client-bounded fee on `AuthCaptureEscrow`. Either breaks "payTo is the seller's verified wallet", and no hosted facilitator lists `auth-capture` today.
   - **A paid listing**: charge to list or to rank. It is a fee on discovery, not on calls, and needs no one in the payment path; it can come later without changing this rail.
7. **Networks: Arc testnet now; Base Sepolia when the asset registry adds it (Kris's decision).** Circle's facilitator settles `exact` on Arc testnet and mainnet and on Base and Base Sepolia, which matches ADR-0002's chains; x402.org's and CDP's settle Base Sepolia but not Arc. The index verifies receipts on the stage's settlement chain only (Arc testnet today, the chain the poller reads). A pay-per-call listing is limited to registered assets, today USDC on Arc testnet; registering Base Sepolia USDC would also let escrow terms name it, so it waits for that decision. Mainnet waits for the roadmap's mainnet step.
8. **The website: discover here, call from your agent or an x402 client.** A listing page shows the price per call, the resource, the `payTo` and whether it is the seller's linked wallet, the verified payments, that there is no refund, and copyable instructions (the agent's `x402_quote` and `x402_receipt`, and the resource's 402 by `curl`). The browser never calls the seller and the page builds no payment; the content security policy stays `connect-src 'self'`, and no edge proxy is added.

## Considered options

- **Sidequest hosts the seller route** (ADR-0001 decision 7). It needs a server that holds the seller's terms and answers on their behalf, an account in all but name; rejected under ADR-0003.
- **Sidequest as the facilitator.** It would put Sidequest's relayer in the payment path and make it liable for screening and failed settlements; the buyer and seller gain nothing a hosted facilitator does not give.
- **A narrow edge proxy for the website's "Pay and call"**: the edge fetches the seller's challenge and forwards the paid call, with the relay pipe's URL rule, size caps and no cookies. It would make Sidequest's edge an open fetcher of any listed URL and put it next to every payment; most sellers send no CORS headers, so a direct browser call fails anyway. Rejected; it can come back once a real wallet (Nod) pays from the page.
- **Verify a receipt with a chain read at ingest.** Not order-independent: the answer depends on when the receipt arrived (before confirmation it is false, later true), and a rebuild would ask again. Rejected for the fact table.
- **Trust the seller's signed receipt** (the offer-and-receipt extension). It proves the seller's word, not the money; its wire shape is unstable. It may be read later as a second label.
- **A `pay` scope for agents.** An agent's key holds no money; spending belongs to Nod's delegations.

## Consequences

- New index tables (migration `0014_x402_rail.sql`): two listing columns, `x402_listing_versions` and `x402_receipts` derived from events, `x402_tx_checks` and `x402_transfers` from the chain.
- A receipt names one transfer; a buyer who pays through a facilitator that batches (Circle's Gateway Nanopayments) has no transaction of their own, so their receipt stays unverified.
- A receipt is verified against the payer's link as it is now: a wallet relinked to another key later unverifies its old receipts.
- Two receipts naming the same transaction count once per listing.
- **Not proven**: a real facilitator (Circle's, on Arc testnet) and a real seller; the end-to-end test uses a reference seller (`examples/x402-seller`, not deployed) and a facilitator written for the test, on Anvil with a test token that adds `transferWithAuthorization` to commerce-payments' ERC-3009 mock. Circle's keyless trial would let a testnet run settle without an account.
